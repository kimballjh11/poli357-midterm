The exam at a glance

5 IDsFrom a list. Define, explain, compare and contrast, with a focus on effects on the Middle East.
1 essayChoose 1 of 2 prompts. Take a position and argue it with examples from class material.
500 wordsMaximum essay length. That is roughly a thesis paragraph plus three tight body paragraphs.
46 termsOn the guide, many of them listed as pairs that invite comparison.
  • The pairs are a clue. The guide writes several terms as pairs (Sunni/Shi'i, Islamism vs. Islam, Eastern Question and Great Game, Neoliberalism vs. Neoconservatism, Safavid and Qajar). Since IDs ask you to "compare and contrast," expect pairs. Prepare every term with a natural partner.
  • "Effects on the Middle East" is the grading line. Several terms are European (Reformation, Commercial Revolution, Westphalia, Bretton Woods). A definition alone will not earn full credit. You need the bridge to the region.
  • The sample essays reward weighing. Prompt 1 asks why your factors matter more than other factors. You need an alternative explanation and a reason to rank yours above it.
  • Closed book, testing center. Time limit is not on the guide. Check Learning Suite or the Testing Center listing.

Connections

Causal chains to have ready

Each chain links terms across units. Any one of them can anchor an essay paragraph. History shaped these outcomes without fixing them, so each arrow is a pressure, not a guarantee.

The world economy

Commercial Revolution → core/periphery → capitulations → Land Code of 1858 → oil, OPEC, Bretton Woods → rentier state → neoliberal reformHow the region was pulled into a European-centered economy as a raw-material periphery, then gained leverage through oil.

The modern state

Ottoman and Safavid/Qajar empires → defensive developmentalism → Tanzimat → constitutionalism → Kemalism and Reza Shah → Nasser and the Ba'athRulers built stronger, more centralized states to survive European pressure. The same machinery later served authoritarian rule.

Sovereignty and the state system

Reformation → Peace of Westphalia → Eastern Question and Great Game → Sykes-Picot, Hussein-McMahon, Balfour → mandates and protectorates → Cold War and neoconservatismA European rule of sovereign territorial states was extended to the region, mostly on terms set by outside powers.

Identity and nationalism

Religious communities → Osmanlilik → nationalism → Zionism and the Arab Revolt → pan-Arabism and Ba'athism → PLOCompeting answers to the question of who the political community is, after empire.

Religion and politics

Ulama → state absorbs religious functions → Wahhabism and Salafism → Muslim Brotherhood → Islamism → Hamas; sectarianism throughoutIslamism is a modern political response to the modern state, not a survival of the premodern past.

Exam technique

Two templates to memorize

Short ID, about five sentences

  1. What: who, what, when, where, in one sentence.
  2. Core feature: the one thing that makes it distinct.
  3. Why it happened: the pressure or actor behind it.
  4. Effect on the Middle East: the concrete consequence, with an example.
  5. Link or contrast: its partner term or the thread it belongs to.

500-word argument essay

  1. Thesis (about 70 words): your position and your 3 factors, plus the alternative you reject.
  2. Three body paragraphs (about 120 words each): claim, mechanism, one or two dated examples from different countries.
  3. Weighing (about 70 words): state the strongest alternative fairly, then why your factors explain more of the variation.
